    Why it matters. Traffic recorded today can be decrypted later by anyone who builds a large enough quantum computer ("harvest now, decrypt later"). A post-quantum key exchange (ML-KEM, standardised by NIST in 2024, combined with X25519) closes that hole for the connection. Nothing breaks today without it. See Crypto Lifetimes for how long ciphers have lasted.

    What was tested. One TLS handshake per bank from a single US machine, to the website the FDIC lists for it (api.fdic.gov), offering only X25519MLKEM768. A refusal was followed by an ordinary handshake, to tell a server without the group from one stuck on TLS 1.2. Failed connections were retried once.

    What was not. The bank's online-banking login, its app and its back-end systems can sit on different servers, so a "yes" here is about the public website only. The network column comes from the site's DNS name chain and Server header. "Own servers or a smaller host" means none of the known networks or hosts was recognised. The lede's content-delivery networks are Cloudflare, Akamai, Fastly, CloudFront, Imperva, Azure Front Door, Sucuri and StackPath; web hosts and cloud load balancers count as "any other way".
